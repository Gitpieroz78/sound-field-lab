Sound Field Lab

One offline page that shows how sound leaves loudspeakers and fills the space: the molecules of the air, the pressure field, the level map, the directivity. It grew out of the "Sound field of a simple source" animation made for Magu after the Meyer Sound "System Design and Optimization" seminar (20-22 October 2026), and keeps everything he liked in it: one molecule in a million, the highlighted molecule, colour by pressure, "Fit 4 wavelengths", ÷2 / ×2, the time period switch, English, Spanish and Italian.

Open it: https://gitpieroz78.github.io/sound-field-lab/

Or download index.html and open it in any browser: it is a single file and works offline. No installation, no account.

The screen

  • Toolbar (top): five ways of looking at the same field, and three cameras.
  • Transport (bottom left): play / pause, ÷2, frequency, ×2, fit 4 λ, fit the scene.
  • Readings (top left): frequency, period, on-screen cycle, speed of sound, wavelength, impedance ρc, absorption, screen width, the system and, when it is on, the microphone.
  • Side panel: four tabs. Signal, Speakers, Medium, View.
  • Examples (header): sixteen ready-made scenes, from the simple source to the end-fire sub array.
Mode What it shows
Molecules one air molecule in a million, moving back and forth; flat dots from the top, shaded spheres at 45°
Pressure the instantaneous pressure as colour (green +, red −), like a ripple tank
SPL map the level in 3 dB bands, relative to the loudest point, like a prediction software
Surface the pressure as the height of a moving sheet
Air block a cube of air cut open: the wavefronts on its faces

Cameras: Top, 45° (isometric) and Low (grazing). Drag to rotate freely, double-click to go back. Every mode works with every camera.

Delays are computed with the speed of sound of the medium you are in, as a system tech would set them. The processing table lets you change gain, delay, polarity and mute of each box; the layout then becomes custom until you press "Back to the installation defaults".

Loudspeakers: point, 18" sub, self-cardioid sub, 12" full range with 90° horn, line array element, column, dipole, small 4" module (for steered columns). Each card shows its directivity at 2 kHz. The acoustic centre is the middle of the front face, where the main driver sits: the field starts there, and in the 3-D cameras the slice cuts the cabinet at that height.

Flown systems hang. The line array (J) and the steered line are computed in a vertical section: the Top camera becomes Side, and at 45° the slice stands upright with the boxes hanging in it.

Boundary: free field, a wall behind the system, a side wall (a floor in the vertical section), with distance and reflection. Modelled with a mirror-image source.

Medium tab

Air (temperature and humidity), helium, hydrogen, CO₂ (also the cold fog of dry ice), fresh water, sea water, steel, or any speed of sound. The panel gives λ, c, ρ, impedance ρc and the absorption at the current frequency.

The model, and its limits

  • Steady-state sine at one frequency. Every box is a point source with a directivity, a gain, a delay and a polarity; the field is their complex sum.
  • Directivity: cabinet baffle from omni (ka ≪ 1) to front hemisphere (ka ≫ 1); piston (Bessel J1) for the 12" woofer; constant-directivity horn; a 35 cm line source for the line array element in the vertical section; first-order cardioid for the self-cardioid sub.
  • The SPL map uses the true 1/r spreading. Molecules, pressure, surface and block use a softer law so the far field stays visible, as in the original animation.
  • Air absorption: ISO 9613-1. Sea water: Thorp. Speeds of sound: 331.4 + 0.607·T for air (Steve Bush, Audio Physics), Marczak for fresh water, Medwin for sea water.
  • This is a teaching tool, not a prediction tool: no measured loudspeaker data, no room, no diffraction around cabinets. Use MAPP 3D or the manufacturer's software for design.

Checks

The physics was checked with automated tests against independent values: the ISO 9613 absorption coefficients, the end-fire and gradient delays used in the Meyer Sound seminar, cardioid rejection, the steering angle of a delayed line, the +6 dB of a wall close behind a source and the size of a hanging line array box in the vertical section. The page makes no network requests.
